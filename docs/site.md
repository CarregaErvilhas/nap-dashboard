# Site

The static site: sources in `assets/site/`, output in `site/`. The never-break rules (CSP, sinks, rebuild) are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## Assets

`assets/` holds `site/` (the site's sources: `index.html`, `rede.html`, `operadores.html`, the `_head/_header/_footer.html` partials, `app.css`, `boot.js`, `common.js`, `find.js`, `uso.js`, `rede.js`, `operadores.js`), `privacidade.html`, `404.html`, `llms.txt`, `pt_outline.json` (PT outline for the map), `pt_basemap.json` (OSM places + main roads for the map; refresh by hand with `scripts/make_pt_basemap.py`, copied to `site/data/basemap.json`), `favicon.svg` (the mascot: a map pin with a face and the lightning bolt of the group's other mascots; also the header's logo), `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`, `icon-maskable-512.png` (the manifest's icons, drawn by `scripts/make_og_image.py` with the others), `og.png` (the link-preview image, mainland map with a dot per site and the mascot pinning Lisbon; redraw it, `favicon.ico` and `apple-touch-icon.png` by hand with `scripts/make_og_image.py`, which needs a Chromium; `build_site.social_tags` writes the og:/twitter: tags of each page from its `<title>`, description and canonical, with a hash of the image in its URL so X re-fetches it when it changes), `schemas/*.xsd` (DATEX II 3.3 © CEN; ver `assets/schemas/README.md`)

`brand/` is the group's kit, a git submodule of `CarregaErvilhas/Brand` pinned to a release tag (`git submodule update --init` in a new clone or worktree). `build_site.py` copies from it into `site/`, at the same paths, `install.js` ("Instalar app": the markup is in `_footer.html`), the mascots (`grupo/*.svg`) and Nunito with its licence (`fonts/`); `scripts/brand_kit.py` lists them. Don't edit `brand/` or those copies here: change them in Brand and move the pin. Dependabot proposes new releases weekly. When a bump changes a copied file or the group's text, run `python3 scripts/build_site.py` on its branch and commit `site/`: `validate_site.py` fails on a stale copy (it doesn't compare the pages' text). A new site in the kit also needs its mascot in `SITE_FILES` (`refresh.yml`) and its colour in `grupo.css` (`.m-<id>`).

## Pages and behaviour

- Four pages, pure CSS/JS, no external libs except the on-demand "Mapa detalhado" below (the fourth, `explorar.html`, is described below): `index.html` "Procurar" (drivers: search by town/operator/code, "Perto de mim", quick filters, a card per site with one status mark per socket, detail with sockets, operator fees, payment and directions links to Google Maps/OpenStreetMap, canvas map), `rede.html` "Rede" (community statistics: KPIs, news, bar charts as real tables, history, hubs, facts) and `operadores.html` "Operadores" (OPCs: weekly anomaly report per operator parsed from `Agents-outputs/anomalias-results.md` by `build_site.parse_opc_report`, OPC churn, reportable errors).
- `grupo.html` "O grupo" (`grupo.css`, `grupo.js`), linked from the footer: the group's sites, one mascot and one speech bubble each. The order, each site's text and the mascots come from `brand/group.json`, put into the page at build time (`scripts/brand_kit.py`); the title, lead, hero bubble, markup and CSS are this site's. Static text in both languages (`data-lang`); `grupo.js` only wires up the header. A file added to `site/` also goes into `SITE_FILES` in `refresh.yml` (validate_site.py checks it).
- `explorar.html` "Explorar" (`explorar.js`, `explorar.css`): cross-filters over `points.json`. Each list (operator, municipality, power, plug, region, price regime, payment) and the status strip is a chart and a filter; a list ignores its own filter (so it can be widened) and the rest of the page follows the whole selection: KPIs, map (`sites.json` + `outline.json`, framed on the selection), largest sites, fee histogram. "Medir por" switches every list between sockets, sites, MW, occupancy, out of service and mean fee; ratio metrics grey out groups under 10 sockets. Filters live in the URL hash (`#m=occ&city=Lisboa&pw=…`), so a view can be shared. Live status as on the other pages, re-read every 5 minutes while the page is visible. The "Ao longo do tempo" section reads `site/data/history.json` (`scripts/explore_history.py`, DuckDB over `data/archive_cache`, after `archive_history.py`): a daily cube (operator × power class × region, from `snapshots`, backfill included: one reading a day, so only size and out of service) and an hourly cube of the last 14 days of live collection (operator × power class, 4 samples an hour, gaps skipped) for occupancy by hour and by weekday × hour, plus `hourly.back`: one sample per backfill status read (4 to 5 a day, the whole network at the NAP's publication instant), which only feeds the weekday × hour profile so it has no empty hours while the live collector is young. Hours still without data are hatched and labelled "sem dados", never shown as 0 %. They follow only the operator, power and region filters, and the page says so. `build_site.write_history` copies `data/explore_history.json` when the run produced it, keeps the committed file otherwise, and writes an empty one (section hidden) when there is none. Charts are SVG built with `createElementNS`, each with a table view.
- "Ao longo dos anos" on the same page reads `site/data/years.json`, the monthly aggregate of the **whole** AmpereArchive (December 2021 on) written by `scripts/explore_years.py`. The file is its own state: each run adds the finished UTC days it lacks and rewrites it, so `refresh.yml` only needs its 28-day cache, and `build_site.py` never touches it. First build or rebuild, by hand: `explore_years.py --download data/archive_full` (~5 700 files, 260 MB, gitignored) then `--root data/archive_full --rebuild` (~90 s).
- `size`: month-end snapshot per operator × power class × region (sockets, kW, and the per-kWh / per-minute price **published in the NAP**). The snapshot day is the last one within 3 % of the month's median, because the NAP loses whole operators for days and the source switch has five days of doubled sockets.
- `occ`: occupancy and out of service per operator × power class. Every archive read is the whole network at one instant; at most one per quarter of an hour is kept, reads are averaged per (day, hour) cell and the cells are summed, so a month read every 15 minutes and one read 5 times a day are on the same scale. A month needs 120 cells (`needs_cells`) to be drawn.
- `meta`: days, cells, reads and `res_min` (median minutes between reads) per month. `NEEDS_RES_MIN` lists what each statistic needs (`repair_time` 15, `sessions` 5) and `ready` counts the months that qualify: a new statistic that needs precision checks `res_min` and gets every month of live collection for free. Add it as new keys in the cubes (additive sums), bump nothing: missing keys read as 0.
- Attributes are the latest known, not as-of: a site that changed operator counts for the current one in every month. Before 2025 the source (OCPI) had statuses only, so sockets gone by 2025 have operator and power -1 and only show with no filter on.
- **The NAP price is not an operator-fee history.** Against the network model, on many MOBI.E sockets the NAP's per-kWh value differs from the OPC fee: it is the ad hoc price, energy included (0,55 where the fee is 0,13), and in 2026 several operators switched from one to the other (Galp: median 0 → 0,60). The page says so. `fold_model` therefore also stores the model's per-kWh price for the month of each refresh, one pair of keys per kind of price (`MODEL_KEYS`: `mn`/`ms` operator fee, `an`/`as` ad hoc, `dn`/`ds` outside MOBI.E, `fn`/`fs` NAP only). Those clean series start with the first refresh that stored them, and each chart appears once it has two months.
- Rede > Concelhos (`rede.js` `fastCard`/`growthCard`, from `stats.json` `territory`, built by `scripts/territory_stats.py`): the concelho is CAOP's (coordinates in the official polygons), never the NAP's free-text `city` of the "15 concelhos" chart. Concelhos with no socket of 50 kW or more (NAP-declared power, working or not), and sites and sockets per concelho a year ago vs now (`sites_at`, [archive.md](archive.md); sockets first; hidden without the archive).
- Rede > Operadores, "Pagar com cartão bancário" (`cardPayCard`, `stats.json` `card_pay`): at sites with a socket of 50 kW or more, the NAP's `auth_methods` (`creditCard`/`debitCard` only: `nfc`/`pinpad` are undocumented in DATEX II) and OSM's `payment:cards`, side by side and never merged (they disagree on hundreds of sites). AFIR Art. 5 needs the installation date and the TEN-T location, which the NAP lacks, so the page says it is not a compliance measure.
- **Kinds of price.** A socket has up to two of four prices, and they are never averaged or compared with each other, on any page: `opc` (operator fee on MOBI.E, `fees.REGULAR`, **without** energy), `adhoc` (no-contract price on MOBI.E, `fees.AD_HOC_PAYMENT`, **with** energy; many sockets also have a per-minute or flat part), `direct` (outside MOBI.E, `nap`, with energy) and `napfee` (regime `mobie_sem_tarifa`: what the NAP publishes, `nap_fees` `{kwh, min, flat}`, from the model's `napFees`; nobody says whether it includes energy, so the pages say exactly that). The rule lives in `scripts/price_kinds.py` (stats, `explore_years.fold_model`) and in `NAP.prices` / `NAP.priceText` (`common.js`: Procurar's "Sem contrato" column and notes, Explorar's three price metrics and the histogram's kind picker, Rede's "Preço por kWh, por tipo" table from `price_stats.kinds`); `NAP.sessionCost` (`common.js`) prices one session at one kind (flat + €/kWh + every €/min tier over the minutes at a constant kW), for Rede's "Quanto custa uma sessão de 20 kWh, por escalão" table (`rede.js`, from `points.json`: NAP power classes split AC/DC by plug, the socket's NAP power caps the car's, medians count €0; `opc` and `adhoc` only, since `direct` is per kWh and `napfee` is of unknown content). It is front-end only, so `validate_site.py` checks it against hand-computed `SESSION_CASES`. `validate_site.py` runs `price_kinds.kinds` and `NAP.prices` over the same cases and fails if they differ. Verified on the data: where MOBI.E publishes an ad hoc plan the NAP's per-kWh value is always that plan's, otherwise it is the REGULAR fee. So the NAP only adds information where MOBI.E has no tariff, and a NAP value is never "the operator fee" of a socket that has one.
- Sources in `assets/site/`; output in `site/` (committed, what `pages.yml` serves). Data in `site/data/{stats,panels,sites,points,outline,opc}.json`, fetched same-origin (`credentials: 'omit'`) and split by `scripts/site_data.py` so each page loads only what it uses; a key added to `data` that is not listed there lands in `stats.json`, never dropped.
- **Must** sanitize NaN/Inf floats to null before the dump (`clean()` in `build_dashboard.py`): bare `NaN` breaks `JSON.parse`.
- Data is no longer embedded in a `<script>`, so it needs no `<`/`>`/`&` escaping. The rule now: the front end inserts third-party text (OSM names, operator names, community notes, report fragments) **only as text nodes**, through the `el()`/`md()` helpers in `common.js`, never `innerHTML` or similar. `validate_site.py` enforces it. The pre-rendered panels (facts/errs/anom/hubs/churn/news/hist) are baked into the HTML at build time (`<!--PANEL:name-->`) from build-escaped HTML, both languages in `data-lang` blocks.
- CSP (`<meta>`, in `_head.html`; Pages can't set headers): `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'` (no inline scripts); `style-src 'self'` (no `style` attributes: scripts set styles through the CSSOM, panels use classes); `img-src 'self' data:`; `font-src 'self'` (Nunito is served from `fonts/`, no font CDN); `manifest-src 'self'`; `worker-src 'self'` (MapLibre's worker module); `connect-src 'self'` plus the AmpereArchive origin and `maptiles.ocarroeletrico.com`; `base-uri 'none'`; `form-action 'none'`.
- Bilingual PT/EN: `boot.js` picks the language before paint (`?lang=pt|en`, then `localStorage` `nap-lang`, then the browser; PT for `pt*`). Strings live in the `STR` tables of `common.js` plus `N.addStrings` per page (`data-i18n`/`data-i18n-ph`/`data-i18n-title` attrs); data enums are display-mapped at render time, so the data keeps stable PT/code values. `data` carries EN mirrors (`kpis_pt` is the PT one; `kpis` stays EN; `facts/errs/anom/hubs/churn_html_en`) generated via `scripts/dashboard_i18n.py` (phrase MAP + PT→EN number conversion). New fixed PT strings in FACTS/ERRS/ANOM/HUBS/CHURN_HTML/NEWS_HTML **must** get a MAP entry or they render in PT for EN users.
- If a page shows nothing below the KPIs, suspect a JS runtime error (an element id that doesn't exist, a variable out of scope); check the browser console and `validate_site.py`.
- Map: a canvas drawn from `sites.json` (per-site lon/lat) + `outline.json` (PT polygons). Regions are `mainland`/`madeira`/`acores`; site `region` values are `azores` (with a `z`), so the map re-maps `azores`→`acores`.
- "Mapa detalhado" (Procurar's map tools and each site's detail): the streets, only on demand. `find.js` imports `mapa.mjs`, which loads MapLibre GL JS, pmtiles and the Protomaps style generator from `site/vendor/` (as published on npm; `validate_site.VENDOR` pins each sha256 and exempts only those bytes from the sink rule) and the basemap from `https://maptiles.ocarroeletrico.com` (R2 `basemap`): `current.json` names the dated `portugal-AAAAMMDD.pmtiles`, plus `fonts/` (Noto Sans glyph ranges) and `sprites/v4/` (light, dark). The MapLibre map takes the canvas's place and drives the same `view`, so the list follows it; region and +/− buttons and the site's detail move it; the sites are the canvas's own `mark()` shapes as icons. Light and dark styles only: the colour-blind and high-contrast modes keep the light streets, and the sites keep the mode's colours. `basemap-tiles.yml` (monthly) re-cuts Portugal to z14 (~330 MB, under the CDN's 512 MB cacheable size; z15 would be ~690 MB) and uploads it over R2's S3 API, because `cf`/wrangler uploads stop at 300 MB. Its secrets: `R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID`/`R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY` (an R2 token for that bucket only) and the variable `R2_ACCOUNT_ID`. Cloudflare resources are managed with the `cf` CLI. How they are set: [workflows.md](workflows.md#basemap-on-cloudflare-r2).
- "Uso ao longo do tempo" in Procurar's site detail (`uso.js`): stacked bars of the time each socket spent charging / available / out of order / unknown over 24 h, 7 days, 30 days and every month the archive has, one lane per socket (per plug and power above 8 sockets; a single socket can be picked), and a weekday x hour occupancy map below. Data: `data/sites/<xx>.json.gz`, 256 files published with the site (see [archive.md](archive.md)), fetched when a detail opens and keyed by the NAP site id (`sites.json` `oop`, or the id `applyLive` matched, `p.nap_site`). No file: the section hides; a site the file lacks: one line saying so. `uso.js` `shardOf` must hash like `site_history.shard_of`: `validate_site.py` runs both on the same ids. Months with under 2 reads per hour (the replayed history) are drawn lighter and called a sample; an hour with no read is hatched, never 0 %. Every bucket is `[cells, charging, available, down]` in hundredths of a cell, so sockets add up.
- Search and filters on `index.html` run on `sites.json`; point-level data (`points.json`) loads after, for the detail view and the live status. Both take power, fees and the DC voltage filter from the network model, so the detail shows every €/min tier ("0; 0,18 após 30 min"), OSM's power with the NAP's beside it ("22 (NAP: 11)") and the NAP-only issues per socket. Sockets are shown by their EVSE code (`evse`, the NAP's `point_external_id`, `NAP.sockId` in `common.js`), never by `point_id`, which some operators publish as a UUID or a bare number; `point_id` stays the key for live status and the per-site history. The payment filter uses `x.pay` (list of OSM payment methods: App/Cartão/Cartão de membro/Sem autenticação/Dinheiro) joined per-site onto every point row.
- "Perto de mim": the browser asks for the location only when the button is pressed; the coordinates are used on the device to sort and centre the map, never sent or stored.

- Aparência: tema (auto/claro/escuro) e visão (padrão/daltonismo/alto contraste) são `data-theme`/`data-vision` no `<html>`, definidos por `boot.js` antes de pintar (valida contra lista fechada o que lê de `nap-theme`/`nap-vision`; `nap-lang` idem). Cores só por variáveis CSS (`--s-<estado>` para os estados); um estado nunca é só cor: cor + forma + texto. O único pedido a outro domínio é o estado ao vivo (`connect-src` na CSP). `assets/privacidade.html`, `404.html`, os ícones e `llms.txt` são copiados para `site/` pelo `build_site.py`; a página de privacidade descreve os pedidos do site, o `localStorage` e a localização (PT e EN), por isso qualquer pedido ou chave novos têm de entrar lá também.
- Estado ao vivo: ao abrir, `index.html`, `rede.html` e `explorar.html` leem `v1/PT/status.json.gz` do AmpereArchive (sem cookies nem referrer; se vier ainda em gzip, descomprime com `DecompressionStream`) e refaz o que depende do estado: gráficos de estado e ocupação, KPI de ocupação, cores do mapa, cartões e filtro de estado. Factos, erros e novidades continuam da semana. Casa cada ponto pelo `point_id` e por `site_id` == `site_external_id` ou a acabar em `-<site_external_id>` (o arquivo usa o `site_id` do NAP, `EZC-AMD-00051`; casa quase todos, sem ambíguos); ambíguo fica como estava. Só aceita estados da lista DATEX II e ignora ficheiros com mais de 180 min. Depois relê as tomadas partilhadas (`NAP.sharedPass`): uma tomada com `pair` (as tomadas do mesmo local com que partilha a potência, aprendidas pelo `archive_history.py`, ver [archive.md](archive.md)) que está `outOfOrder`/`inoperative`/`blocked` enquanto uma delas carrega passa a `shared`, "Em uso pela outra tomada", que conta como ocupada; o `build_dashboard.py` faz o mesmo ao estado da semana. `shared` é nosso, não do DATEX II, e `estados.html` (`estados.js`, números de `stats.json` `states`) explica cada estado; o ⓘ da legenda do histórico do posto e o painel Histórico ligam para lá. Sem rede, bloqueado ou desatualizado, mostra o estado da semana e diz-o no cabeçalho. `validate_site.py` desliga o fetch (`NAP_NO_LIVE`) e testa `applyLive` com dados sintéticos. O host vem de `<meta name="nap-live-origin">` (`dashboard_payload.ARCHIVE_ORIGIN_DEFAULT`) e o build troca-o, nessa meta e no `connect-src` da CSP, pela origem de `AMPERE_ARCHIVE_URL` (variável do repo, passada ao passo de build); só aceita `https://host[:porta]`, senão avisa e fica o por omissão.
